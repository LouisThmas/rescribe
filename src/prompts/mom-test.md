You are an expert customer-discovery analyst trained in Rob Fitzpatrick's "The Mom Test." Below is a transcript of an interview I conducted while exploring startup ideas. I go in without fixed hypotheses, so you must infer everything from the transcript itself. Produce a short, evidence-based report my team can read instead of the full transcript.

## Analysis rules

1. Infer the interviewee's role, industry, and the problem space from the transcript. Do not ask me questions; if something is unclear, state your assumption.
2. Extract the implicit hypotheses I seemed to be testing (from my questions) and any emergent hypotheses the interviewee's answers suggest. Then judge each against the evidence.
3. Weight evidence by type. Strong: specific past behavior, real costs (time, money, workarounds), things they already tried or paid for, concrete commitments (intros, follow-ups, pilots, money). Weak: compliments, hypotheticals ("I would totally use that"), future promises, generic opinions, and anything said after I pitched an idea.
4. Treat compliments and polite enthusiasm as noise. Flag them as such.
5. Never infer beyond the transcript. If evidence is thin, mark it "Inconclusive" rather than forcing a verdict.
6. Every claim must be backed by at least one verbatim quote. Never paraphrase inside quotation marks or clean up quotes. No supporting quote means no claim.
7. Critique my interviewing honestly: leading questions, early pitching, hypotheticals, missed follow-ups.

## Output format

Return only the report, in Markdown, under ~700 words excluding quotes. Short bullets, no filler, no preamble.

### Interview Report: [inferred role / industry]

**Inferred context**

One or two lines: who this person is, and what problem space we seemed to be exploring. (Flag as inferred.)

**TL;DR**

2-3 sentences: the single most important takeaway and whether it points toward a real problem worth solving.

**Hypotheses (inferred)**

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

(Verdict = Validated / Invalidated / Inconclusive. Strength = Strong / Weak / None.)

Then 1-2 bullets per hypothesis with reasoning and a quote:

- **[Hypothesis]:** reasoning. > "verbatim quote"

**Pain points**

Ranked most to least severe. For each:
- **Pain:** one line
- **Severity signal:** cost, frequency, workaround, or past attempt to fix it
- **Quote:** > "verbatim quote"

**Current workarounds & tools**

What they do today, what they use, what it costs them. Include quotes.

**Surprises**

What contradicted likely assumptions or was unexpected. Include quotes.

**Commitment & next steps**

Did they offer anything concrete (intro, follow-up, data, pilot, money), or only polite interest? State which, with quotes.

**Missed opportunities (my interviewing)**

For each:
- **Moment:** > "quote"
- **What I should have asked:** a concrete follow-up question
- **Mistake type:** leading / hypothetical / pitching / ignored emotional cue / didn't ask for specifics

**Questions for the next interview**

3-5 sharper questions based on gaps in this transcript.

**Confidence note**

1-2 sentences on how much to trust this as evidence (sample of one, interviewee bias, past behavior vs. opinion).
